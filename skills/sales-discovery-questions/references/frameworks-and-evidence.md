# Frameworks and evidence

The four questioning frameworks with a publicly documented structure, what each one costs and buys as a coverage lens, and the honest state of the evidence. Rows below are ordered by efficiency; the selection logic ranks them in this same order.

## The four frameworks, in efficiency order

| Framework           | Origin                                                                      | Structure                                                                                                                    | Costs (call minutes, rehearsal, buyer patience)                                                                                                                                  | Buys                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| GAP Selling         | Keenan, 2018                                                                | Current state → future state → gap; PIC chart: Problem / Root Causes / Impacts; impact split technical / business / personal | An hour of per-deal rehearsal; two impact questions inside a stage already open                                                                                                  | Root-cause-before-impact ordering, the three impact dimensions, and the quantified figure everything downstream runs on; the logistics-first negative examples                                               |
| Sandler Pain Funnel | Sandler (sequence published by a vendor; Sandler's own pages don't show it) | Vague → specific example → duration → prior attempts → cost → emotional weight                                               | The same hour of rehearsal and the same handful of minutes - ties GAP on effort; spends the most goodwill per stage, since every rung asks the buyer to stay on one sore subject | Depth on one pain: the ladder that separates a complaint from a problem, inside the pain and impact stages                                                                                                   |
| SPIN                | Neil Rackham, 1988 book                                                     | Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff                                                                              | Near-zero - re-sequences questions already written, adds none                                                                                                                    | Near-zero on top of the spine, which has already absorbed its macro ordering: context before problem, problem before consequence. Free, never budgeted for                                                   |
| MEDDPICC            | Originated at PTC, 1990s (Dick Dunkel); popularized by MEDDICC (Andy Whyte) | 8 elements incl. Metrics, Identify Pain, Decision Process; the Three Whys: "Why anything? Why you? Why now?"                 | Highest: a whole extra stage, re-gathered per persona across the cycle, and minutes spent on process rather than on pain                                                         | Decision-context coverage and the Three Whys for urgency - the path by which a quantified pain reaches someone who can approve it. Convergence, not any single acronym, is the skill's spine: establish the situation, isolate a real problem, dig to root cause, quantify impact across several dimensions, then establish why now.

## Applying the chosen lens

Step 4 already decides which lens and which ones get deleted; this section only covers how the survivor is applied.

- Mandated methodology → map every stage to its vocabulary (e.g. MEDDPICC shop: label impact questions "Implicate the Pain", metrics questions "Metrics") so the call notes land in their CRM fields.
- Decision-context answers feed `meddpicc-scorecard` downstream wherever that stage survives; this skill never scores.
- Challenger-style outbound → not a fifth lens but a contingency on top of whichever one you kept: open pain with a point-of-view statement to react to. Insight-led selling de-emphasizes questions up front, it doesn't remove the need to diagnose.

## Evidence caveats - read before quoting any number

- SPIN's research base ("35,000 sales calls") is proprietary and was never published for independent replication; Rackham himself publicly acknowledged skepticism about the figure. Treat SPIN as a well-established practitioner ordering, not a proven law.
- The "ideal question count" is a vendor claim:
  - One conversation-intelligence vendor (Gong) published 11-14 questions as correlating with success.
  - Its own 2025 refresh (519,000+ calls) reported won deals at ~15-16 questions and lost deals at ~20.
  - The rule breaks down against C-suite buyers.

  Correlational, non-auditable corpus, never independently replicated. Coach the behavior - fewer, deeper, evenly spread questions - never the number.

- The "43:57 talk-to-listen ratio" (same vendor, 2016, n≈25,500 calls) was revised by the vendor's own 2025 data to a much smaller gap (~57% rep talk on won vs ~62% on lost). Same caveats. Use talk-listen only as a directional coaching signal.
- Independent peer-reviewed work supports the general premise, not the numbers: a 2011 meta-analysis (Verbeke, Dietz & Verwaal, Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science) found adaptiveness and selling-related knowledge among the strongest performance predictors. Treat any specific question-count or ratio threshold as unvalidated by that literature.
- Buyer-indecision findings (Dixon & McKenna, published book research on ~2.5M conversations): a large share of deals end in no-decision, and added pressure worsens indecision - the basis for this skill's "record the absence of a compelling event honestly" rule. Book-published vendor-adjacent research; same replication caveat.
- Buying-group size:
  - A Gartner survey (2017, n=750, Gartner's own figure) puts the median B2B buying group at 6-10 decision makers.
  - A 2024 Forrester survey reports averages around 13.

  Analyst figures from paid research - directionally solid, precise numbers vary by study.

- If a claim appears elsewhere and has no traceable source listed here or in the question bank, use "not verified" rather than asserting it.
